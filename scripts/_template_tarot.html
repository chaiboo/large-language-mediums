Which beliefs chatbots argue with

“Tony, Tony, turn around, something’s lost and must be found,” offers Claude Sonnet 4.6 when asked how I can pray to retrieve my grandmother’s lost ring. The same model, when prompted to advise me on how I may clear my energies for the same task, answers that it would be doing me a disservice if it gave me crystals-and-meditation advice. I had not mentioned crystals. There is a substantial discrepancy between how models dispute the same questions when they are framed through practices that are culturally protected (established religious traditions, prayer, etc.) and those that don’t enjoy the same cultural privileges (astrology, energies, pendulums, magic, etc). This study seeks to measure the gap and whether that line follows the familiar distinction between religion and its others.

The design involves forty-eight questions asked five different ways (a secular/plain version, protected and unprotected framing of the same question then a “testable” claim, again through unprotected and protected framings) to eight different artificial intelligence models.

The questions were a mix of everyday scenarios (e.g. setting a wedding date, accepting a job offer, scheduling flights, launching a new business venture, disputing a security deposit with the landlord) and high stakes situations (e.g. stopping chemotherapy, skipping addiction treatment, vaccinating children, overinvesting in cryptocurrency, dealing with an impending home foreclosure).

The pairs

The columns are the five prompts of one item. A vermilion border means the model disputed the claim, gold that it hedged, violet that it answered straight. Every prompt was answered five times, and the numbered buttons on each card switch between the five answers, each button edged in its own verdict's color.

Method

  • Corpus: decision questions, five prompts per item, half everyday and half consequential (chemotherapy, a toddler's vaccines, home birth against medical advice, savings on a poker hand).
  • Prompts: a testable claim through a practice, the practice with no claim, or no practice at all, question held constant.
  • Protected practices: prayer, prophecy, novenas, felt leadings. Unprotected: astrology, tarot, numerology, pendulums, mediums, psychics.
  • Responses: the prompts re-answered by eight models over OpenRouter, temperature 0.7, five draws apiece ({%run_day%}).
  • Errors: {%n_errors%} of {%n_expected%}, all but one Qwen refusing sensitive prompts; {%n_kept%} judged, {%n_total%} double-labeled.
  • Judging: GPT-4o on answered, in_frame, challenged, distanced, refused, then Gemini-2.5-Flash blind on all of them (kappa on disputes: {%kappa%}; {%kappa_tp%} on the protected claim, its lowest cell).
  • Labels: disputed is challenged or refused; hedged is complied with a caveat. The unprotected claim holds {%n_dis_tu%} of {%n_disagree%} disagreements.
  • Judges: neither is in the test set, but GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-3.5-Flash share their labs (kappa {%k_gptmini%} and {%k_gemflash%} on those rows, against {%k_claude%} for Claude-Sonnet-4.6).
  • Coverage: Qwen2.5-72B carries {%qwen_lo%} to {%qwen_hi%} responses per cell against {%cell_n%} (kappa {%k_qwen%}); excluding it shifts no headline rate by a point.
  • Prompts: authored minimal pairs, not user data.
